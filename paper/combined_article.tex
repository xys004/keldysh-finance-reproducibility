\begin{abstract}
We give a finite-scale account of how temporal organisation amplifies the
variance of signed market order flow.  The presentation is designed to make
the translation between market microstructure and nonequilibrium statistical
physics explicit.  Four years of hourly raw signed volume and
volume-normalised imbalance are analysed in four primary cryptocurrency assets,
with four additional assets kept as a post-hoc extension.  The central result
is an exact finite-sample decomposition of weekly variance into an analytic
marginal reference, sign ordering at calendar-homogenised magnitudes, and a
sign--magnitude remainder.  Under the primary quarterly $\times$ hour-of-week
conditioning, raw variance is amplified by $2.18$--$3.32$ and the coupled
remainder accounts for $65$--$79\%$ of the excess; paired eight-week
moving-block intervals remain above one half in all four primary assets.  The
attribution is scale-conditioned: monthly strata lower the coupled share to
$45$--$49\%$, and two-point biweekly strata make it unidentifiable.  Global
variance growth remains superdiffusive under drift-resistant estimators,
$\nu=1.10$--$1.36$, while a single-power correlation model fails and the local
crossover is unresolved.  We also identify an apparent post-event ageing
signal that is reproduced by randomly placed events and caused by pre-event
normalisation.  Exact second-order and Gaussian identities are separated from
measurements throughout, so neither accumulated variance nor a linear
fluctuation asymmetry is counted as independent evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Market orders have a direction and a volume.  A buyer-initiated trade removes
liquidity from the ask; a seller-initiated trade removes liquidity from the
bid.  Summing their signed volumes over a time window produces a net flow.  In
the language of statistical physics this is a current, and the sum over a
finite window is a transferred quantity.  The vocabulary is useful because it
brings response functions, noise, counting statistics, and finite-time nulls
into one calculation.  It does not turn a market into an electronic conductor
or convert signed volume into entropy production.

Order-flow persistence is well established
\cite{lillo2004,bouchaud2004,bouchaud2018}.  Order splitting explains how a
large parent order can generate a long same-sign sequence, and account-level
data support this mechanism quantitatively
\cite{lillo2005,sato2023prl,sato2023prr}.  Recent work treats signed flow
together with fluctuating volume, volatility, and price impact
\cite{maitrier2026,muhlekarbe2026}.  Our question is narrower: at a specified
horizon and calendar conditioning, what fraction of the excess variance is
carried by sign ordering at a typical flow magnitude, and what fraction
requires signs and magnitudes to retain their joint temporal organisation?

That distinction cannot be obtained by comparing two marginal
autocorrelations.  If $x_t=s_tm_t$, every off-diagonal contribution to the
variance contains $s_ts_{t'}m_tm_{t'}$.  Nor can two asymmetric surrogate
tests be read as additive components.  We instead construct a finite-sample
identity with an analytic reference and an explicit interaction remainder.
The result is the main empirical contribution of this article.

Three supporting results delimit its interpretation.  First, accumulated-flow
variance and the flow autocorrelation are exactly the same second-order
information written in two forms.  Second, a linear
$\log[P(Q)/P(-Q)]$ is automatic for a Gaussian variable and therefore is not,
by itself, a fluctuation theorem.  Third, local scaling and post-event clocks
are sensitive to mean drift and normalisation.  We use drift-resistant
estimators, moving-block uncertainty, and random-event placebos to separate
what is measured from what is an identity or an unresolved diagnostic.

\section{Data and observables}
\label{sec:data}

We use public Binance candles for BTC, ETH, BNB, and SOL against USDT over four
years.  Each hourly candle records total base volume $V_t$, taker-buy base
volume $V_t^+$, and trade count.  We analyse
\begin{equation}
 \eps_t^{(r)}=2V_t^+-V_t,
 \qquad
 \eps_t^{(n)}=\frac{\eps_t^{(r)}}{V_t},
 \label{eq:flows}
\end{equation}
with $\eps_t^{(n)}=0$ when $V_t=0$.  The first is raw signed base volume; the
second is dimensionless imbalance in $[-1,1]$.  A candle aggregates many
trades, so $|\eps_t|$ below is a magnitude of hourly residual flow, not the
size of an individually identified market order.

The complete data contain $35\,063$ hourly observations per asset.  The weekly
analysis retains $206$ complete Monday--Sunday UTC weeks, or $34\,608$
candles.  XRP, ADA, DOGE, and AVAX form a post-hoc extension selected after the
primary analysis was fixed.  They broaden the empirical panel but do not enter
the original four-asset decision rule.

We checked the candle construction against public individual-trade archives
for one complete UTC day: $9{,}797{,}860$ BTCUSDT and $4{,}969{,}357$ ETHUSDT
operations.  Aggregation reproduces hourly volume, taker-buy volume, and trade
count to floating-point precision.  The individual trades also show why an
absolute Fano level is not central here.  The median individual sizes are
smaller than the median hourly mean-size proxies by factors $87.5$ and $71.6$.
Moreover, a compound-Poisson process already has a Fano factor proportional to
$\avg{q^2}/\avg{q}^{2}$, which can be large without memory.  Ratios and the
analytic reference introduced below avoid this arbitrary quantum.

Public candles do not reveal parent metaorders or trader identities.  Results
about order splitting are therefore mechanistic interpretations informed by
the literature, not direct account-level measurements.  This distinction is
especially important because impact inferred from public flow can misassign
the source of order-flow autocorrelation \cite{naviglio2025}.  Our central
variance calculation concerns the public flow series itself and does not
reconstruct metaorder impact.

All windows are trailing, flow features are aligned to the candle in which
they are observed, and response is referenced to the opening rather than the
closing price.  Perturbing future observations leaves past features unchanged;
these alignment and no-look-ahead properties are tested in the public code
\cite{code_keldysh_finance}.

\section{Memory and aggregate response}
\label{sec:memory-response}

We use one sign convention throughout:
\begin{equation}
 C_\eps(\tau)\sim \tau^{-a},\qquad a>0.
\end{equation}
The raw hourly flow does not support one exponent over all resolved lags.
Over $\tau\in[2,10]$, the fitted fast exponents are
$a_f=1.00\pm0.15$, $0.87\pm0.08$, $0.46\pm0.05$, and
$1.71\pm0.23$ for BTC, ETH, BNB, and SOL.  Over $\tau\in[10,50]$, the slow
exponents are $a_s=0.17\pm0.13$, $0.22\pm0.09$, $0.29\pm0.05$, and
$0.16\pm0.32$.  Removing the daily seasonal mean changes neither regime
materially [Fig.~\ref{fig:base}(b)].  The slow component is therefore not a
nondecaying daily drive, although its exponent remains imprecise.

The lagged aggregate response
\begin{equation}
 R(\tau)=\frac{\operatorname{cov}(p_{t+\tau}-p_t,\eps_t)}{\Var(\eps_t)}
\end{equation}
is much flatter than $C_\eps$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:base}(a)].  This is consistent
with the standard ``surprise'' mechanism: prices respond primarily to the
innovation in persistent flow rather than to its predictable component
\cite{gerig2008,jaisson2014,taranto2016,muhlekarbe2026}.  Some series have a
small but resolved response slope, so ``flat'' is an approximation and not a
universal law.

An earlier derivative ratio $-C'_\eps/R$ was too noisy to support an empirical
fluctuation--dissipation claim and has been removed from the result set.
Differentiating a sample correlation and dividing by an estimated response
requires joint uncertainty that the data do not resolve.  Nothing below uses
that ratio.

\section{Counting fluctuations and exact safeguards}
\label{sec:counting}

For a window of $T$ candles define
\begin{equation}
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\eps_t,\qquad
 s_T(Q)=\log \frac{P(Q_T=Q)}{P(Q_T=-Q)}.
\end{equation}
The notation comes from full counting statistics
\cite{levitov1993,levitov1996,blanter2000,esposito2009}; here it simply means
the distribution of accumulated market flow.

Two identities prevent double counting of evidence.  First,
\begin{equation}
 \Var(Q_T)=\sum_{t,t'=1}^{T}C_\eps(t-t')
 =T C_\eps(0)+2\sum_{\tau=1}^{T-1}(T-\tau)C_\eps(\tau).
 \label{eq:variance-covariance}
\end{equation}
Thus variance growth and the autocorrelation are two representations of one
second-order object, not independent confirmations.

Second, if $Q_T$ is Gaussian with mean $m_T$ and variance $V_T$, then
\begin{equation}
 s_T(Q)=\frac{(Q+m_T)^2-(Q-m_T)^2}{2V_T}
 =\frac{2m_T}{V_T}Q
 \label{eq:gaussian-identity}
\end{equation}
exactly.  A measured linear fluctuation asymmetry with slope
$2\avg{Q_T}/\Var(Q_T)$ therefore has a Gaussian algebraic explanation.  It is
not sufficient evidence for a Gallavotti--Cohen theorem
\cite{gallavotti1995,lebowitz1999}.  The data are not uniformly Gaussian:
standardised skewness and excess kurtosis do not converge to zero across all
assets and scales.  We consequently use Eq.~\eqref{eq:gaussian-identity} as a
null explanation, not as a fitted distributional model.

There is also no established current large-deviation principle.  The usual
speed is $T$, whereas the variance here grows as $T^\nu$ with $\nu>1$.  We do
not infer entropy production, thermodynamic affinity, or a market temperature
from $s_T(Q)$.

\section{Exact sign--magnitude attribution}
\label{sec:attribution}

This section contains the central measurement.  Complete weeks are demeaned
within a declared period $\times$ UTC hour-of-week stratum $g(t)$, giving
$x_t=s_tm_t$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 Q_w=\sum_{t\in w}x_t,\qquad V_{\rm obs}=\Var_w(Q_w).
\end{equation}
Every complete week contains at most one observation from each stratum.  Under
independent within-stratum permutations, all expected off-diagonal weekly
products vanish, while the diagonal sum is preserved.  The exact expectation
of the joint-order null is therefore
\begin{equation}
 V_0=\frac{1}{W-1}\sum_t x_t^2.
 \label{eq:analytic-null}
\end{equation}
This replaces a Monte Carlo denominator by a finite-sample identity.

To isolate temporal sign organisation on the same calendar footing, center
the signs and homogenise only the magnitudes in the off-diagonal term:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s_t=s_t-\avg{s}_{g(t)},\qquad
 \bar m_{g(t)}=\avg{m}_{g(t)},
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
 C_s&=\sum_w\sum_{\substack{t,t'\in w\\t\ne t'}}
 \widetilde s_t\widetilde s_{t'}
 \bar m_{g(t)}\bar m_{g(t')},\nonumber\\
 V_s&=\frac{C_s}{W-1},\qquad V_{sm}=V_{\rm obs}-V_0-V_s .
 \label{eq:attribution}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:attribution} reconstructs $V_{\rm obs}$ exactly.  The
fractions
\begin{equation}
 f_s=\frac{V_s}{V_{\rm obs}-V_0},\qquad
 f_{sm}=1-f_s
\end{equation}
are reported without clipping.  The interaction allocation is conditional on
sign centering and stratum-wise magnitude homogenisation.  The observed
diagonal remains in $V_0$, so $V_0+V_s$ is not the ordinary variance of the
homogenised proxy.  Other declared stratum scales would produce other exact
remainders.  We therefore present a fixed-diagonal operational attribution,
not an orthogonal or uniquely causal decomposition of a market mechanism.

With quarterly $\times$ hour-of-week conditioning, raw-flow amplification is
$2.75$, $3.32$, $2.18$, and $2.53$ for BTC, ETH, BNB, and SOL
[Fig.~\ref{fig:coupling}(a)].  The sign shares are $0.21$, $0.30$, $0.35$, and
$0.35$; the coupled shares are $0.79$, $0.70$, $0.65$, and $0.66$.  Paired
eight-week moving-block intervals for $f_{sm}$ are $[0.63,0.88]$,
$[0.62,0.78]$, $[0.52,0.76]$, and $[0.55,0.74]$.  The coupled remainder is
therefore a majority in all four primary raw point estimates and throughout
their intervals.

Normalised imbalance has $f_s=0.41$--$0.51$ and $f_{sm}=0.49$--$0.59$.
Normalisation raises the sign-share point estimate in every primary asset, but
paired intervals resolve the increase clearly only for BTC.  This is a
directional consistency observation rather than an independent universal
test.

The attribution is scale-conditioned.  Monthly strata lower the primary raw
coupling share to $0.45$--$0.49$.  A 14-day partition leaves at most two
observations per hour-of-week stratum; after demeaning, their residual
magnitudes are equal and $V_{sm}$ is forced to zero.  That case is
unidentifiable, not evidence that coupling vanishes.  The quarterly--monthly
contrast places much of the resolved interaction in multiweek organisation
absorbed by finer conditioning.

The post-hoc raw coupling shares are $0.52$--$0.73$, but two of four intervals
cross one half.  We use them to support a non-negligible interaction, not an
eight-asset majority claim.  Cross-asset weekly correlations also show that
the series are not independent replications.  Finally, the original
predeclared gate required raw observed variance to exceed twice the 98.75th
joint-null percentile in all four primary assets; BNB failed.  The exact
attribution answers a different question and does not rewrite that outcome.

Uncertainty uses 3999 paired circular moving-block replicates with block
lengths 4, 8, 13, and 26 weeks.  The same resampled week indices enter all
components, and stratum estimates are held fixed.  Full tables, deposited
outputs, and reconstruction checks are available in the public repository
\cite{code_keldysh_finance}.

\section{Scaling: robust level, unresolved crossover}
\label{sec:scaling}

For a single kernel $C(\tau)\sim\tau^{-a}$ with $0<a<1$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:variance-covariance} gives $\Var(Q_T)\sim T^{2-a}$.  At $a=1$
the leading form is $T\log T$; for $a>1$ the correlation is summable and the
asymptotic variance is diffusive.  The domain distinction matters: the
uncertainties of the BTC and SOL fast exponents cross $a=1$, so a simple
$\nu=2-a$ band is not uniformly defined for them.

A broad-window single-power prediction misses all eight primary
asset--resolution series [Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling}(a)].  Predicted exponents are
$1.45$--$1.88$, while block estimates over $T=1\ldots256$ are
$1.22$--$1.37$.  Restricting the block fit to $T=8\ldots256$ gives
$1.31$--$1.43$; the two ranges refer to different grids and are not
interchangeable.  Two-regime point-estimate bands are only consistency checks
because their amplitudes and crossover uncertainty were not fitted.  BNB's
$\nu=1.37$ lies outside its $[1.54,1.71]$ band
[Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling}(b)].

The apparent local exponent rises with scale in the block estimator, but a
drifting block mean contributes a $T^2$ term with the same qualitative
signature.  We therefore compare block variance with Daubechies filters having
one, two, and three vanishing moments.  Applied directly to the flow, db1
removes a constant mean, db2 a linear trend, and db3 a quadratic trend
\cite{daubechies1992,percival2000}.  All four estimators agree within $0.01$ on
exact fractional Gaussian noise of known $\nu$; injected drift moves only the
estimator that should be sensitive.

On the data, drift-resistant estimates span $1.10$--$1.36$
[Table~\ref{tab:scaling-estimators}], below the block values in every asset.
Thus global superdiffusion is robust, but the local crossover is not resolved.
At $T=256$ only $135$ non-overlapping windows remain in the complete-week
sample, making the longest scales the noisiest.  BNB also turns over at the
long end instead of continuing the proposed monotone drift.  A longer record
or a stable-mean asset would be needed to discriminate the crossover.

\section{Post-event clocks and a normalisation artefact}
\label{sec:clocks}

We define shocks as absolute returns above the 99.5th percentile and construct
240 hourly post-shock trajectories.  The mean normalised absolute return is
compatible with relaxation, but an Omori-amplitude fit gives
$\beta=0.61\pm1.01$ and six of ten asset--resolution fits terminate at an
optimiser boundary.  The uncertainty includes zero, so no universal relaxation
exponent is identified [Fig.~\ref{fig:clocks}(a)].

The correlation time appears to grow monotonically with waiting time, with a
Spearman coefficient of $+1.00$.  Repeating the entire procedure at randomly
placed event times returns the same median coefficient
[Fig.~\ref{fig:clocks}(b)].  The apparent ageing is therefore produced by the
measurement convention: dividing a post-event signal by a noisy pre-event
scale injects a slow common mode as the process moves away from that reference.

This is a ratio-normalisation problem related to classical spurious-correlation
bias \cite{pearson1897,kronmal1993,ciuparu2016} and to event-conditioned
correlation distortions \cite{forbes2002,boyer1999}.  In a granular cooling
state, normalised two-time correlators similarly exhibit apparent ageing from
self-similar scale decay even without memory \cite{brey2007}.  Random-event
placebos are therefore necessary whenever waiting time and normalisation share
the same event origin.  Our result is limited to this construction; it does not
invalidate ageing measured with a different estimator.

\section{A compact stochastic representation}
\label{sec:representation}

The empirical results can be organised without a microscopic market field
theory.  Let the demeaned flow have a Wold representation
\cite{wold1938,hamilton1994}
\begin{equation}
 \eps_t=\sum_{k\ge0}h_k w_{t-k},\qquad
 \avg{w_tw_{t'}}=\sigma_w^2\delta_{tt'},\qquad h_0=1,
 \label{eq:wold}
\end{equation}
where $w_t$ is the innovation not linearly predictable from past flow.  A
minimal price equation
\begin{equation}
 p_{t+1}-p_t=\lambda w_t+\eta_t
\end{equation}
immediately gives a lag-independent covariance between future price change and
present flow for positive lags.  This is the familiar surprise model, stated
here only to connect the nearly flat response to finance language.  It is a
benchmark consequence of the assumed innovation coupling, not new evidence.

If the flow were Gaussian, adding a counting field $\chi$ would give
\begin{equation}
 F_T(\chi)=\log\avg{e^{\chi Q_T}}
 =\chi m_T+\frac{1}{2}\chi^2V_T.
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:variance-covariance} and
\eqref{eq:gaussian-identity} then follow immediately.  This compact
representation clarifies the logical status of the calculations: the response
and Gaussian identities are closures under declared assumptions, whereas the
sign--magnitude attribution and its intervals are measurements.

\section{Inference rules used in the analysis}

Three practices materially changed the conclusions.

\emph{Paired resampling.}  Ratios built from the same weeks are resampled with
the same block indices.  Marginal confidence intervals do not answer whether
two dependent components differ.

\emph{Positive controls.}  A null result is accompanied by an
injection--recovery test on synthetic data carrying the proposed signal.
Otherwise ``no effect'' is indistinguishable from an insensitive pipeline
in a null analysis.

\emph{Multiplicity at scanned scales.}  In an exploratory grid of 144 related
configurations, the smallest nominal $p$ value was $2\times10^{-4}$, yet the
Westfall--Young maximum-statistic correction gave a global $p=0.43$
\cite{westfall1993}.  Scale scans are therefore treated as model diagnostics,
not as 144 independent opportunities for discovery.

All numerical claims in the text are generated from deposited JSON outputs;
the code checks exact reconstruction, fixed seeds, cache hashes, and no
look-ahead.  The archived outputs also preserve failed predeclared gates and
withdrawn exploratory analyses rather than replacing them with later choices
\cite{code_keldysh_finance}.

\section{Discussion}

The main result is not that signs dominate.  At quarterly conditioning, sign
ordering at homogenised magnitudes explains only $21$--$35\%$ of the primary
raw weekly excess.  The remaining $65$--$79\%$ requires the actual temporal
organisation of signs with hourly flow magnitude.  This is consistent with
order splitting, in which a parent order can generate both a same-sign run and
elevated executed flow, but the public candle data do not identify that
microscopic mechanism directly.

The word ``finite-scale'' is essential.  Monthly conditioning absorbs enough
low-frequency organisation that the coupled share is no longer a resolved
majority, and biweekly conditioning destroys identifiability.  The result is a
property of a declared observable, horizon, venue, and conditioning scheme,
not a universal constant of markets.

The same discipline applies to the other sectors.  Global superdiffusion is
robust under drift-resistant estimators, but the detailed crossover is open.
The event-conditioned correlation does not establish ageing because its
placebo produces the same statistic.  The accumulated variance is not
independent of the autocorrelation, and a Gaussian linear fluctuation
asymmetry is an identity.  Stating these boundaries makes the positive result
easier to see: the resolved empirical content is a large, scale-conditioned
sign--magnitude contribution to finite-window order-flow noise.
